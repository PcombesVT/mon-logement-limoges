Limoges, a city that's good to live in

The capital of the Haute-Vienne and the Limousin, Limoges is a human-scale city that combines economic energy, a unique heritage and remarkable quality of life — at one of the lowest costs of living in France.

The city

A dynamic regional capital

Limoges is the capital of the Haute-Vienne and the largest city of the Limousin. Crossed by the Vienne and nestled between forests and hills, it offers a quality of life that many larger French cities envy: green spaces, smooth transport, almost no traffic jams, affordable property.

With over 18,000 students and a teaching hospital that serves the wider region, Limoges is a real university and medical city. Each year it draws thousands of newcomers — young professionals, families, remote workers — won over by its exceptional quality-of-life-to-cost-of-living ratio.

Two and a half hours from Paris by high-speed train, Limoges stays connected without being swallowed up by big-city sprawl. That's exactly what makes it a city where people settle… and stay.

What makes Limoges

A city of many faces

Porcelain, industry, medicine, food, nature in the city… Limoges is far more than its reputation. Here's what newcomers quickly discover.

🏺

Limoges porcelain

Since the 18th century, Limoges has been synonymous with fine porcelain. The discovery of kaolin at Saint-Yrieix-la-Perche in 1768 changed everything. Haviland, Bernardaud, Royal Limoges… the great manufacturers made the city famous worldwide. The Adrien Dubouché national museum holds one of the finest ceramics collections in Europe.

⚡

Legrand, a global home

Founded in Limoges in 1860, Legrand is today the world leader in electrical infrastructure. Its headquarters have stayed in the city — on Avenue du Maréchal Fayolle — making Limoges one of the few industrial players of this scale still rooted in a mid-sized city. Legrand employs several thousand people in the Haute-Vienne.

🏥

A leading teaching hospital

Limoges teaching hospital is the department's top employer, with over 6,000 staff. A leading facility for the Limousin, the Creuse and part of the Corrèze, it draws doctors, nurses, health students and paramedical staff. The Faculty of Medicine, the IFSI and the midwifery school complete this major health hub.

🎓

A university city

The University of Limoges brings together 7 faculties and grandes écoles: medicine, law, arts (FLSH), sciences, pharmacy, ENSCI (ceramics), ENSIL-ENSCI (engineering). Over 18,000 students bring the city to life, especially in the Vanteaux and La Borie districts. A vibrant, affordable student life.

🍽️

Limousin food

Limousin beef and veal are world-renowned premium cattle breeds. The region also produces Clochard apples, walnuts, chestnuts, wild mushrooms and black truffle. The markets of Limoges — the central covered market, the Motte market — are busy all year round. And the Limousin clafoutis remains a must.

🌿

Nature and green spaces

Limoges is one of the greenest cities in France. The botanical garden, Victor-Thuillat park, the banks of the Vienne, the ponds… the people of Limoges have nature on their doorstep. Thirty minutes away by car, the first lakes and forests offer hiking, fishing, swimming and time to recharge.

History

A thousand years of history & craftsmanship

From the Romans to Legrand, by way of the medieval enamellers and the 18th-century porcelain makers — Limoges has an industrial and craft history of rare richness.

Antiquity
Augustoritum, a Gallo-Roman city

Limoges was founded by the Romans under the name Augustoritum. Capital of the Gaulish Lemovices tribe (which gave the city its name), it quickly became an important road junction in Gaul.

12th–14th century
The enamellers of Limoges, a European renown

The art of enamelling made Limoges a major artistic centre of medieval Europe. Limousin shrines, reliquaries and enamelled plaques adorned the cathedrals and royal treasuries of England, Spain and Italy.

1768
The discovery of kaolin, the birth of porcelain

The discovery of pure kaolin at Saint-Yrieix-la-Perche, 40 km from Limoges, radically transformed the city's economy. The first hard-porcelain manufacturers set up, soon rivalling Sèvres and the Saxon workshops.

1842
Haviland settles in Limoges

David Haviland, an American merchant, settled in Limoges to produce the porcelain he exported to the United States. His factory revolutionised techniques and designs, making Limoges a global name.

1860
Legrand is founded

Frédéric Legrand founded a maker of trinkets and porcelain in Limoges. The company later pivoted to the emerging electrical industry and, over the decades, became the world leader in electrical infrastructure, while keeping its headquarters in Limoges.

1929
Bénédictins station, an Art Deco masterpiece

Opened in 1929, Bénédictins station is one of the most beautiful stations in France. Its dome, towers and classical-Art Deco façade make it an architectural symbol of the city, visible for miles.

Today
A city reinventing itself

Limoges combines its industrial heritage with new ways of living: co-working spaces, French Tech, the timber industry, cutting-edge medicine, cultural tourism. A city in transition, drawing more and more remote workers and families in search of a balanced way of life.

Economy

The economic pillars of Limoges

Limoges isn't just an attractive housing market — it's also a city with a solid, diverse economy and stable jobs.

6 000+

Limoges teaching hospital

The department's top employer. A leading university hospital for the whole Limousin, growing fast since the opening of the new mother-and-child hospital.

~5 000

Legrand — global headquarters

The world leader in electrical infrastructure, listed on the CAC 40. Its headquarters are in Limoges. Several factories and R&D centres employ thousands of people in the Haute-Vienne.

~18 000

University of Limoges

7 faculties, 18,000 students, hundreds of academics and administrative staff. The university is a leading economic and cultural driver for the whole city.

PME

Industrial & craft fabric

Porcelain (Bernardaud, Royal Limoges), footwear (a heritage), timber and forestry, printing, food processing… Limoges has a solid, diverse fabric of SMEs and craftspeople.

Quality of life

Moving to Limoges: the real reasons

Newcomers to Limoges often share the same surprise: "We didn't think it was this pleasant." Less stress, more space, neighbours who know each other, and a city where you can do everything on foot or by bike.

Families appreciate the right-sized schools, accessible sports facilities and general safety. Young professionals discover a lively cultural scene — the Zénith, the Grand Théâtre, festivals, arthouse cinemas.

  • Public transport (STCL buses) covering the whole area
  • Growing cycle paths, a calmer city centre
  • Many parks, accessible banks of the Vienne
  • The Halles covered market of Limoges, 7 days a week
  • The Zénith, the Grand Théâtre, national museums
  • Average commute times under 15 minutes
